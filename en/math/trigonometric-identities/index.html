Trigonometric Identities

Printable summary of the most-used trigonometric identities: Pythagorean, sum and difference, double-angle, half-angle formulas. Free to print. Free to print.

This sheet collects the trigonometric identities that come up most often in algebra, geometry and physics, grouped by the kind of problem they solve rather than by difficulty. Angles are written as α, β and θ, and every line holds for any angle. Use it as a lookup while working: start from the group that matches the shape of the problem in front of you, then work back towards the Pythagorean identities if you need something simpler to substitute in.

Pythagorean

  • sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
  • tanθ = sinθ / cosθ
  • 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
  • 1 + cot²θ = csc²θ

Sum / difference

  • sin(α ± β) = sinα cosβ ± cosα sinβ
  • cos(α ± β) = cosα cosβ ∓ sinα sinβ
  • tan(α ± β) = (tanα ± tanβ) / (1 ∓ tanα tanβ)

Double angle

  • sin2α = 2 sinα cosα
  • cos2α = cos²α − sin²α = 2cos²α − 1 = 1 − 2sin²α
  • tan2α = 2tanα / (1 − tan²α)

Half angle

  • sin²(α/2) = (1 − cosα) / 2
  • cos²(α/2) = (1 + cosα) / 2

How to use it

  • Start from the Pythagorean identities - most of the others can be derived from them if you forget one.
  • When a problem mixes two different angles, reach for the sum and difference group; when it doubles or halves one angle, use the double-angle and half-angle groups.
  • Watch the ∓ and ± signs: they flip between the top and bottom lines of a sum or difference formula.

Frequently asked questions

What is a trigonometric identity?

An equation involving trigonometric functions that is true for every angle. Unlike an equation you solve, an identity is a fact you can substitute into a problem to make it simpler.

Which identities do I actually need to memorise?

In practice, sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 and the sum and difference formulas cover most exam questions. The double-angle and half-angle formulas can be derived from those.

What do the letters α, β and θ mean?

They are conventional names for angles, exactly like using x for an unknown. Which letter is used makes no difference to the formula.

Why do some formulas use both ± and ∓?

The two signs move together but in opposite directions. When the left side uses +, the matching term on the right uses −, and the other way round.

What is the difference between an identity and an equation?

An equation asks you to find the angle that satisfies a condition and has at most a handful of solutions; an identity is a relation that holds for every angle. That is why identities can be substituted into problems to simplify them, rather than solved.

Do these identities work in degrees or radians?

Both, because the identities are algebraic relations between trigonometric functions rather than numerical values of angles. Convert to whichever unit your problem uses, then apply the identity without changing it.

Which identity should I start with if I am stuck?

Almost always sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. It is the one identity that turns a square of a function into a simpler expression, which then unlocks most of the rest.
