Hundreds Chart (1–100)

Printable hundreds chart from 1 to 100 in a 10×10 grid, plus a blank 10×10 grid for fill-in practice. A4 and Letter friendly, free. Free to print, no signup.

Math

A hundreds chart is a 10-by-10 grid that lists the numbers from 1 to 100, row by row. It is one of the most-used early-grade classroom resources for kindergarten through grade 2, because every pattern children need in skip-counting and basic arithmetic is visible on a single sheet. Reading down a column shows the tens digit increasing by one while the ones digit stays the same. The blank grid underneath lets a child fill in numbers they are still learning, and is the reason to print this page rather than use a generic worksheet.

How to use it

  • Start at 1 and read every number in order, either row-by-row or column-by-column — column reading is usually the harder and more useful one.
  • Practice skip-counting by 2s, 5s and 10s: highlight every 5th cell to show the count by fives, then do the same for twos and tens.
  • Use the blank grid to ask the child to write in a missing number. Pick a cell they cannot guess at random by asking for the number that sits between, say, 27 and 29.
  • Print at 100% rather than "fit to page" so the 10×10 cells stay square — this matters for visual pattern work.

Frequently asked questions

What is a hundreds chart?
It is a 10-by-10 grid listing the numbers from 1 to 100, row by row. It is one of the most-used early-grade math resources because it shows place value, skip-counting and simple arithmetic patterns all at the same time.
What age or grade is a hundreds chart for?
It is most often used in kindergarten through second grade (ages 5 to 7) for counting to 100 and the basic patterns around it, and then again in grade 3 and 4 for multi-digit addition and subtraction practice.
Why is a hundreds chart useful for skip-counting?
Reading down any column shows how counting by 2s, 5s or 10s repeats the same pattern — twos skip a cell, fives skip four cells, tens skip nine cells — so the chart turns an abstract counting pattern into something a child can see and point at.
What is the blank grid for?
It turns the chart into a fill-in worksheet. A child fills in missing numbers, or counts by 2s, 5s or 10s and writes the numbers in the right cells, instead of looking at a finished chart and reading off an answer.
Does a hundreds chart work on A4 and Letter?
Yes. The grid is small enough that both sheet sizes leave comfortable margins around it without rescaling; print at 100% scale, not "fit to page", so the cells stay square.
