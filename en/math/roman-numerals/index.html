Roman Numerals Chart (1-3,000)

Printable Roman numerals chart listing the seven basic symbols, the subtractive combinations for 4, 9, 40, 90, 400 and 900, and example values. Free. Free.

Roman numerals use the letters I, V, X, L, C, D and M as digits, with smaller symbols in front of larger ones meaning subtract. This chart lists the seven symbols, the six subtractive combinations that cover every number from 1 to 1,000, and the example values 1,999 and 3,000 so a reader sees the pattern keep going. They still appear on clock faces, on building cornerstones, and in the names of monarchs and popes. Children meet them most often when reading dates on older buildings, when learning about Roman history, and when looking at the credits of older films.

How to use it

  • Start by learning the seven symbols and their values, then learn the six subtractive combinations (IV, IX, XL, XC, CD, CM).
  • Read any Roman numeral left to right: add the value of a symbol unless the next symbol to the right is larger, in which case subtract.
  • Check your reading with the table: 1,999 is MCMXCIX, and the pattern keeps going for any larger year.
  • Practise reading years: pick a year and write it in Roman numerals, then check it against a list of real dates.
  • Practise writing the years of your own birth, school start and any recent event in Roman numerals as a small daily exercise.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Roman numeral?

A way of writing numbers using the letters I, V, X, L, C, D and M, with smaller symbols in front of larger ones meaning subtract.

Why does the chart list combinations like IV and IX?

Because Roman numerals use a subtractive rule: 4 is written IV (5 − 1), not IIII. The six combinations cover every value from 1 to 1,000.

Why does the chart stop at 3,000?

Past 3,000 the symbols keep repeating the same pattern, so the chart does not need to. MMM is the largest value without using overlines.

Where do Roman numerals still appear?

On clock faces, on building cornerstones, in the names of monarchs and popes, in film credits, and in the suffixes of Super Bowls.

Can I use Roman numerals for calculations?

In principle yes, but the position rules make arithmetic slow and error-prone. Today they are used for display, not for calculation.

What is the largest Roman numeral you can write without overlines?

MMM, which is 3,000. Past that, an overline (vinculum) multiplies the value beneath it by 1,000, so V̅ with an overline would be 5,000. The chart stops at MMM because the overline convention is rare in everyday use.

How do I write the current year in Roman numerals?

Break the year into thousands, hundreds, tens and ones, then translate each piece with the table. 2026, for example, is MMXXVI.
