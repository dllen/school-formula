Area & Volume Formulas

Printable geometry formulas summary: area, perimeter and circumference for 2D shapes, and volume and surface area for 3D shapes. Free to print. Free to print.

This chart groups the geometry formulas most often needed in elementary and middle-school work into four groups: area of 2D shapes, perimeter and circumference of 2D shapes, volume of 3D shapes, and surface area of 3D shapes. Each line is a single formula with the variable letters spelled out, so it can be used without first going back to the textbook. The formulas are listed in the same order as they appear on a geometry test, so the chart works as a memory aid as well as a reference.

Area (2D)

  • Rectangle: A = l × w
  • Square: A = s²
  • Triangle: A = ½ × b × h
  • Circle: A = π × r²
  • Parallelogram: A = b × h
  • Trapezoid: A = ½ × (a + b) × h

Perimeter / Circumference

  • Rectangle: P = 2 × (l + w)
  • Square: P = 4 × s
  • Triangle: P = a + b + c
  • Circle: C = 2 × π × r

Volume (3D)

  • Cube: V = s³
  • Rectangular prism: V = l × w × h
  • Cylinder: V = π × r² × h
  • Cone: V = (1/3) × π × r² × h
  • Sphere: V = (4/3) × π × r³
  • Pyramid: V = (1/3) × B × h

Surface area

  • Cube: SA = 6 × s²
  • Rectangular prism: SA = 2 × (lw + lh + wh)
  • Sphere: SA = 4 × π × r²
  • Cylinder: SA = 2 × π × r × (r + h)

How to use it

  • Group the formulas by the shape they apply to: rectangle formulas live with rectangles, prism formulas with prisms, and so on.
  • Always write the variable names next to every number while computing a value - most mistakes here are unit mistakes, not arithmetic ones.
  • Use the chart to double-check a formula you have just derived: a typo in a constant (like π vs 2π) is easier to spot when the formula is printed next to a list of alternatives.
  • Pair the chart with the metric-conversions page when computing surface area in unfamiliar units.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the chart group formulas by shape?

Because the formula a child needs depends on the shape they are working with. Grouping by shape lets them jump straight to the right block rather than scanning a long list.

What is the difference between area and perimeter?

Area is the number of unit squares that fit inside a shape, while perimeter is the total length of its outline.

Why is volume 1/3 × base × h for a pyramid or cone?

Because a pyramid or cone fits inside a prism or cylinder of the same base and height, and occupies exactly one third of that prism.

Why is the surface area of a sphere 4πr² rather than πr²?

A sphere has four "great circles" of area πr² wrapped onto its surface, that is the same as the area of the circle that inscribes it, times four.

Does this chart include formulas for irregular shapes?

No. The chart covers the standard named shapes. For irregular shapes, the formula is usually broken into triangles or other standard pieces.

Why is the volume of a triangular prism "area of base × height"?

Because a prism is a stack of identical cross-sections; multiplying the cross-section area by the stack height counts the layers.

Why is the surface area of a cube 6 × s²?

A cube has 6 square faces, each with area s². The formula 6 × s² adds the areas of all six identical faces.
