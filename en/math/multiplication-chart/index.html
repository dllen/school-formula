Multiplication Chart (1-12)

Printable 1-12 multiplication chart with every times table, handy for homework and home practice. Borders not shading, so it prints in black and white. Free.

This 1-12 multiplication chart puts every times table on a single grid. Find the row for one number and the column for the other, and the cell where they meet is the product - so 7 × 8 is the number where row 7 meets column 8. Printing it and keeping it on a desk or in a homework folder gives children a fast way to check their own work instead of reaching for a calculator. It is also a compact way to notice the patterns that make times tables easier to remember: the diagonal of square numbers, the symmetry either side of it, and the easy 10s column.

How to use it

  • Put one finger on the row for the first number and one on the column for the second, then read the cell where they meet.
  • Read down the 10s column first - it is the quickest win, and it makes the rest of the grid less intimidating.
  • Print at 100% rather than "fit to page" so the grid stays square and easy to scan.

Frequently asked questions

What is a multiplication chart?

A grid that lists the product of every pair of numbers from 1 to 12. The row and the column each stand for one factor, and the cell where they meet is the answer.

Is this the same as a times table?

Yes. A chart shows all the times tables at once; a times table usually means one of the individual lines, such as the 7 times table.

Why does this chart stop at 12?

Twelve is the range most schools expect children to know by heart. Charts that go higher are useful for spotting larger patterns, but 12 covers the facts that come up in everyday arithmetic.

Can I print it in black and white?

Yes. The grid uses borders rather than shading, so it stays readable on a mono printer.

What patterns should I look for?

Every product is mirrored across the diagonal of square numbers, and the 10s, 11s and 5s columns run in obvious sequences. Spotting those three columns first turns the rest of the grid into something to predict rather than to learn cell by cell.

Does this help with long multiplication?

Yes, in two ways. Knowing the single-digit products cuts the time spent working them out, and the grid shows which double-digit products like 11 × 12 or 12 × 12 are still single cells, so they become the answer rather than another step to break down.
